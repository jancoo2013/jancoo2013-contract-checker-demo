## Error hypotheses, not fabricated model failures

The five cases expose distinct **potential mistakes** for a later independent evaluation: equating an open cheque with unlimited authority; missing late handwritten changes; assuming an instrument automatically expires or continues without checking renewals; treating the words “security cheque” as bank blocking; and confusing a filled/presented instrument with a proved, matured underlying claim. Each hypothesis is an engineering inference inspired by documented litigation, **not** an observed model failure, general legal rule, certified training label or held-out Gold answer.

Two judgments concern leases entered after the 2017 residential reform; three predate it. Do not transfer an earlier contractual or bill-of-exchange holding to the current statutory scheme without separately verifying applicability, enactment and later interpretation. Distinguish the three questions: bank presentment, opening an execution file, and substantive right to realize security. A small-claims or magistrates decision is not a general binding verdict for unrelated facts.
