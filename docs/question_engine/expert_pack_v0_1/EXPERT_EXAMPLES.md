# Expert Pack v0.1 — Expert Examples

Status: experimental contrastive demonstrations.

These are sanitized/synthetic minimal examples distilled from observed model failure modes. They are not quotations from a real contract and are not statutory or market-practice authority.

The purpose is to teach the boundary between a tempting reading and a source-grounded expert reading.

## E01 — Return rule belongs to the stated instruments

TEXT:
- §5.3.1: security cheque, 20,000 NIS, no date stated.
- §5.3.2: open utility cheques; these are returned within 30 days after proof of no debt.

WRONG:
"The 20,000 NIS security cheque is returned within 30 days."

WHY WRONG:
The return sentence is grammatically attached to the utility instruments.

EXPERT READING:
For the 20,000 NIS security cheque: amount known; date status stated separately; return mechanism not found in these fragments.
For utility cheques: return deadline = 30 days subject to the stated proof condition.

PRINCIPLE:
Do not transfer a rule between different instruments.

## E02 — Contract silence is not a physical blank

TEXT:
The contract requires delivery of a security cheque but does not name its payee.

WRONG:
"The cheque has a blank payee."

WHY WRONG:
The contract describes an obligation but does not show the physical cheque.

EXPERT READING:
Payee in contract text = not established. Physical payee field = unknown unless separately observed.

PRINCIPLE:
Do not infer physical instrument state from missing contract text.

## E03 — Unrelated 90-day period

TEXT A:
On sale of the apartment, a party must provide 90 days' notice.

TEXT B:
Early exit is governed by a replacement-tenant mechanism.

WRONG:
"The tenant may exit with 90 days' notice."

WHY WRONG:
The 90-day number belongs to the sale/transfer mechanism.

EXPERT READING:
Early-exit notice period is not established by TEXT A. Analyze TEXT B on its own terms.

PRINCIPLE:
Every number must remain attached to its actual mechanism.

## E04 — Specific replacement route versus general prohibition

TEXT A:
The tenant remains liable until the end of term unless an acceptable replacement tenant is introduced under the stated process.

TEXT B:
Assignment and subletting are generally prohibited.

WRONG:
"Replacement tenant is prohibited."

WHY WRONG:
A specific replacement route may operate as an exception or separate mechanism.

EXPERT READING:
Read A and B together. Determine whether the replacement route is an explicit exception to the general prohibition and what approval standard applies.

PRINCIPLE:
Test specific rule against general rule before declaring conflict.

## E05 — Preserve inconsistent payment descriptions

TEXT:
One section says 12 cheques.
A later line enumerates 3 + 3 cheques.
Another line requires monthly bank deposit.

WRONG:
"Rent is paid by 12 monthly cheques."

WHY WRONG:
The source contains three payment descriptions and has not resolved them.

EXPERT READING:
Preserve all three facts. Mark the payment mechanism internally inconsistent or unclear until another clause resolves the relationship.

PRINCIPLE:
Do not normalize contradictions into the most plausible scheme.

## E06 — Specific cure is not universal cure

TEXT:
Rent arrears may be cured within 7 days after the stated notice.
Other breaches are discussed elsewhere without that 7-day wording.

WRONG:
"All breaches have a 7-day cure period."

WHY WRONG:
The cure period is tied to a specific breach category.

EXPERT READING:
Rent-arrears cure = 7 days under the stated procedure. Cure for other breaches = separately determine; do not import 7 days.

PRINCIPLE:
Keep clause-specific procedures scoped to their trigger.

## E07 — Broad fundamental breach plus special procedure

TEXT A:
Several listed violations are defined as fundamental breach.

TEXT B:
For rent arrears, the contract provides notice and a 7-day correction period before the stated next step.

WRONG:
"Any listed fundamental breach causes immediate termination."

WHY WRONG:
The broad classification does not erase a special procedure for one listed breach.

EXPERT READING:
Map the fundamental-breach definition and the rent-specific cure rule together. The practical effect for rent arrears is narrower than the broad definition alone suggests.

PRINCIPLE:
Classification and remedy procedure are separate parts of one mechanism.

## E08 — AS-IS must be read with repair allocation

TEXT A:
Tenant confirms inspection and accepts the dwelling AS-IS.

TEXT B:
Landlord remains responsible for stated system failures and specified pre-existing/hidden defects.

WRONG:
"The tenant accepts all defects and all repair responsibility."

WHY WRONG:
TEXT B qualifies the apparent breadth of TEXT A.

EXPERT READING:
Record the AS-IS acknowledgment, then narrow its practical meaning using the repair and defect exceptions actually stated.

PRINCIPLE:
Second-pass clauses can narrow an initial concern.

## E09 — Holdover is part of an end-of-tenancy chain

TEXT:
The landlord may inspect 21 days before return.
The tenant has 10 days to correct listed defects.
Remaining after the return date triggers double daily compensation.

WRONG:
"The contract imposes double compensation if the apartment is not in perfect condition."

WHY WRONG:
The holdover trigger concerns remaining after the return date; the defect process is a separate but related pre-return mechanism.

EXPERT READING:
Read inspection, correction, return date, and holdover together. Do not merge the defect-correction process into the holdover trigger.

PRINCIPLE:
Related mechanisms must be connected without collapsing distinct triggers.

## E10 — Option details unresolved does not mean no option

TEXT:
The contract mentions a tenant option, but rent for the option period is stated to depend on a referenced addendum that is not provided.

WRONG:
"There is no renewal option."

WHY WRONG:
The option is present; part of its economics is unresolved.

EXPERT READING:
Option mechanism present. Economic dependency unresolved because the referenced document is missing.

PRINCIPLE:
Distinguish mechanism absence from incomplete mechanism.

## E11 — Nearby clause number is not evidence

TEXT:
§8.2 discusses repairs.
§8.3 discusses landlord access.
A later sentence says "as stated in §8.3" while describing repair reimbursement.

WRONG:
"§8.3 establishes repair reimbursement."

WHY WRONG:
The reference may be broken or mistyped; §8.3 must be checked for subject match.

EXPERT READING:
Verify the referenced clause. If it does not support the reimbursement rule, preserve a broken/internal-reference issue instead of using the neighboring number as proof.

PRINCIPLE:
Validate references semantically, not just numerically.

## E12 — Missing optional clause is not a finding

TEXT:
The contract contains no building-insurance clause and does not reference one.

WRONG:
"Missing building-insurance clause is a contract defect."

WHY WRONG:
Absence of an optional/wishlist provision is not a source-grounded problem by itself.

EXPERT READING:
No finding unless another existing mechanism depends on that clause or a separate verified rule layer makes it material.

PRINCIPLE:
Do not convert an ideal-contract checklist into contract facts.

## E13 — Referenced missing document is different

TEXT:
The lease states that the inventory in Appendix B forms part of the agreement.
Appendix B is not included in the supplied package.

WRONG:
"The inventory terms are normal and can be assumed."

WHY WRONG:
The referenced source is missing.

EXPERT READING:
Appendix B is a document dependency. Analysis of obligations tied to that inventory is incomplete until the referenced document is available.

PRINCIPLE:
A referenced missing dependency is first-class uncertainty.

## E14 — No invented market judgment

TEXT:
Security cheque amount = 20,000 NIS.
Monthly rent = 5,000 NIS.
No comparative source layer is supplied.

WRONG:
"Four months of rent is above the normal market range."

WHY WRONG:
The contract supplies the numbers, not a market benchmark.

EXPERT READING:
State the amount and ratio if useful. Do not label the amount standard, unusual, excessive, or typical without a separate verified comparison/rule layer.

PRINCIPLE:
Contract arithmetic is allowed; unsupported market judgment is not.

## E15 — Multiple security instruments stay separate

TEXT:
The contract requires a security cheque, two utility cheques, and guarantor undertakings.
Only the utility-cheque paragraph states a 30-day return rule.

WRONG:
"All security is returned within 30 days."

WHY WRONG:
The instruments have different text and may have different lifecycle rules.

EXPERT READING:
Create a separate mechanism record for each instrument. Apply the 30-day rule only where the contract links it. Mark return mechanics for the others as not found unless another clause supplies them.

PRINCIPLE:
Instrument identity is a hard boundary unless the contract explicitly links mechanisms.
